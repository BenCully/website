What it means for buyers

  • Repos flow through wholesale auctions first, so supply shows up at dealers with a lag of months — watch the Manheim index for softening. So far it hasn't: the official October index rose 0.5% to 206.8 (+2% YoY, against a weak October 2025), wholesale supply was a normal 28.1 days, and Cox expects wholesale prices to end 2026 only about +0.2% YoY (Cox, Nov 6 and Q3 release, Oct 7).
  • Where softness is showing: light-duty trucks fell 4.3% YoY in October wholesale (non-adjusted), and luxury, pickups and SUVs are all below last year, while compact cars are up 6.5% and EVs 6.6% (Cox, Nov 6). Gas fell to $4.07 a gallon in early November, and Cox expects cheaper fuel to slowly revive demand for full-size trucks and SUVs (Cox, Nov 9), so the truck discount may not last. Retail used inventory was just under 50 days' supply in late October (49 in September, when cars under $15k had just 34 days), so cheap cars are still scarce.
  • More supply is coming off lease: Cox expects off-lease volume to rise from about 190,000 a month in the first half of 2026 to about 220,000 a month in the second half, and negative equity means more of those cars go to auction instead of being bought out (Cox Q3 2026 presentation).
  • Lenders are feeling it: in Q3 2026 GM Financial's loan-loss provision rose to $340M from $244M, Ally's retail auto net charge-offs were 1.89%, and subprime lender Credit Acceptance swung to a $56.6M GAAP loss as loan performance worsened. Credit Acceptance's provision jumped 170% to $410.9M as it cut forecast collections by $291.2M, yet its loan volume grew 51% (CURepossession, Nov 4), and TransUnion says subprime auto originations grew 9.8% YoY (Nov 5). Rising lender losses usually mean more repossessed cars at auction in the months ahead.
  • Most likely bargains: 2021–2023 model-year compacts, crossovers and full-size trucks bought at peak prices.
  • Low subprime recovery (38%) means lenders are dumping cars cheaply at auction — dealers may capture that, not you; negotiate.
  • Caution: Philadelphia Fed research suggests delinquent loans are staying unresolved longer rather than surging, so a sudden "flood" isn't guaranteed [2]; the American Recovery Association likewise says lenders are holding delinquent accounts 90–120+ days before repossessing, and Manheim repo-volume growth has slowed to 9% (Auto Wire, Oct 21 2026). Fitch notes tax refunds boosted used-vehicle demand and recoveries in spring 2026 before fading into summer [3].

Forecast formula

Formula: P(t) = P0 × (1 + d + m + f)^t, with t = months from Oct 2026 (CarEdge prices re-fetched Nov 9, 2026). P0 = CarEdge's current average used price for the 3-year-old 2023 model year (2024 for the Trax, which has no 2023 entry; the Envista has no CarEdge price, so it isn't forecast). d = monthly aging depreciation = (1 − a)^(1/12) − 1, where a = 1 − (1 − dep5)^(1/5), from CarEdge's 5-yr depreciation. m = market drift = (MUVVI Oct 26 ÷ Oct 25)^(1/12) − 1 = (206.8/202.88)^(1/12) − 1 = 0.160%/mo (updated Nov 9 from Cox's official October value; it was −0.047%/mo on Nov 2 using Sep 26 ÷ Sep 25, so every 12-month base case is now about 2.0–2.4 points higher. Note that October 2025 was unusually weak, which flatters this YoY ratio). f = flood adjustment = −0.2%/mo × (score − 50)/50, an assumption of about ±2.4%/yr at the extremes. Band = base × (1 ± 1.2816·σ·√t), where σ = 1.27% is the standard deviation of monthly MUVVI changes from Oct 2023 to Oct 2026 (roughly an 80% band). These are wholesale-index-driven retail estimates for a car that keeps aging, not price quotes.

Method & verification

Flood score formula

Scoring formula (estimate): Score = 100 × (0.30·D + 0.20·S + 0.25·L + 0.10·R + 0.15·A)
D = min-max normalized CarEdge 5-yr depreciation % (model-level). S = normalized Cox new-vehicle days' supply: model-level where Cox published it (Ram 1500, Grand Cherokee, Wrangler, RAV4, Camry), otherwise brand-level (Sep 2026 where published, incl. Lincoln 127 and Mini 132 via Ford Authority and Chevrolet 88 via GM Authority; Aug 2026 for Nissan 67, Kia 69, BMW 78, Hyundai 82, Infiniti 86, Mitsubishi 87, VW 90, Mazda 92, Mercedes-Benz 97, Audi 97 and Chrysler 104, from Cox/vAuto data in TVB's September snapshot, added Nov 9; note the August industry figure was 73 vs 82 in September, so August brand values run a bit low by comparison); brands with no published figure (Tesla, Alfa Romeo) use the industry's 82 days, flagged. L = loan stress: percentile rank (0–1) across the 67 models of a tier-adjusted 60+ day delinquency rate (by loan balance), from 412,865 active loans in the Sep 2026 SEC ABS-EE loan-level filings of 15 auto ABS trusts (AmeriCredit 2024-1, Santander Drive 2024-2, Drive Auto 2024-1, Exeter 2024-2, CarMax 2024-2, Carvana 2024-P2, Ford Credit 2024-A, Hyundai 2024-A, Nissan 2024-A, Toyota 2024-A, Bridgecrest 2024-1, Ally 2024-1, World Omni 2024-A, Capital One Prime 2024-1, Honda 2024-1). Why adjust: carmakers' own prime lenders (Ford, Honda, Hyundai, Nissan, Toyota) hold most of their brands' loans, which would make those brands look safer just because of who financed them. Method: trusts are grouped into three lender tiers (subprime; prime non-captive; prime captive). Within each tier where the vehicle has ≥200 loans, its relative risk = (observed + 5) ÷ (expected + 5), where observed/expected are delinquent loan-equivalents at the vehicle's vs. the tier's overall rate (the +5 shrinks small samples toward 1). Relative risks are averaged with equal tier weights (not by loan counts), and the adjusted rate = average relative risk × the equal-weight average of the three tier-wide rates (prime captive 0.44%, prime non-captive 1.61%, subprime 9.54%). R = repo share: same method on repossessedIndicator. Vehicles with no tier ≥200 loans use their make's adjusted figures (flagged); a make with none gets the median (flagged). Raw pooled rates stay in each "Why?". Limits: results depend on which trusts were sampled (2024-vintage pools); equal tier weights are a choice, not a measured US lending mix; small samples remain noisy; and delinquency reflects who lenders approved for each car, not only the car itself. Westlake, GLS and Flagship trusts are 144A deals with no public ABS-EE, so they can't be added. LoanTape's make-level recovery flags are kept as context text only. A = affordability proxy (1 − normalized newest-model-year new price, CarEdge). This assumes cheaper cars have more subprime buyers; Experian shows subprime is 20.6% of used and 6.88% of new financing (Q1 2026) but has no figures by model. Price direction: ↓↓ score ≥60, ↓ 45–59, → below 45. Loan APR/term and incentive data are not inputs.
