What it means for buyers

  • Repos flow through wholesale auctions first, so supply shows up at dealers with a lag of months — watch the Manheim index for softening. So far it hasn't: the official October index rose 0.5% to 206.8 (+2% YoY, against a weak October 2025), and Cox expects wholesale prices to end 2026 only about +0.2% YoY (Cox, Nov 6 and Q3 release, Oct 7). Early November is soft but seasonal: Black Book's wholesale values fell 0.66% in the week ending Nov 7, with cars (−0.75%) weakening faster than trucks and auction conversion down to 56% (Black Book, Nov 10). The mid-November Manheim reading is due Nov 18.
  • Where softness is showing: light-duty trucks fell 4.3% YoY in October wholesale (non-adjusted), and luxury, pickups and SUVs are all below last year, while compact cars are up 6.5% and EVs 6.6% (Cox, Nov 6). Manheim says wholesale values of cars rated above 40 mpg are up 9.9% since January while those under 15 mpg have lost 15% (Business Insider, Nov 15). Retail used inventory was 49 days' supply in October, but cars under $15k had just 34 days and are 19% scarcer than a year ago (Cox, Nov 13), so cheap cars are still scarce.
  • New-car lots are fuller: October new-vehicle supply rose to 87 days (83 in Sep), with Jeep 159, Ram 148 and Dodge 147 vs Toyota 39 (Cox, Nov 12). Heavy new-car supply means discounts on new models that pull down used prices for the same brands, so those brands score higher here. More supply is coming off lease too: Cox expects off-lease volume to rise from about 190,000 a month in the first half of 2026 to about 220,000 a month in the second half (Cox Q3 2026 presentation).
  • Borrowers are stretched: 5.66% of all US auto loan balances were 90+ days late in Q3, the highest in NY Fed data back to 2003 (Nov 10). KBRA's non-prime 60+ index rose to 6.70% in October (Nov 13). Lenders keep adding risk anyway: subprime was 17.9% of October loans and 58.7% carried negative equity (Dealertrack, Nov 11), while new-car loan rates hit about 10.5% and used rates near 15% (Cox, Nov 16). Lender losses are rising: Credit Acceptance's Q3 provision jumped 170% to $410.9M, GM Financial's rose to $340M, and buy-here-pay-here chain America's Car-Mart is running on short lender waivers (latest through Nov 16, Clairmont, Nov 14). Rising lender losses usually mean more repossessed cars at auction in the months ahead.
  • Most likely bargains: 2021–2023 model-year compacts, crossovers and full-size trucks bought at peak prices.
  • Low subprime recovery (38%) means lenders are dumping cars cheaply at auction — dealers may capture that, not you; negotiate.
  • Caution: Philadelphia Fed research suggests delinquent loans are staying unresolved longer rather than surging, so a sudden "flood" isn't guaranteed [2]. The NY Fed's flow into serious auto delinquency (3.02%) is barely above a year ago, and Cox notes most borrowers are keeping up. The American Recovery Association says lenders are holding delinquent accounts 90–120+ days before repossessing, and Manheim repo-volume growth has slowed to 9% (Auto Wire, Oct 21 2026).

Method & verification

Flood score formula

Scoring formula (estimate): Score = 100 × (0.30·D + 0.20·S + 0.25·L + 0.10·R + 0.15·A)
D = min-max normalized CarEdge 5-yr depreciation % (model-level). S = normalized Cox new-vehicle days' supply, October 2026 (Cox/vAuto, published Nov 12): model-level where Cox published it (Grand Cherokee 149, Wrangler 155, Silverado 1500 99, F-150 93, CR-V 43, Camry 28, RAV4 24; Ram 1500 keeps its September model figure of 142 because Cox gave none for October), otherwise brand-level from Cox's October brand chart (e.g. Jeep 159, Ram 148, Dodge 147, Buick 136, Lincoln 130, Mini 126, Audi 125, Chrysler 120, VW 118, Ford 112, Infiniti 112, GMC 110, Mitsubishi 106, Hyundai 100, Mercedes-Benz 100, Mazda 96, Chevrolet 94, Nissan 89, Kia 82, BMW 81, Subaru 62, Honda 57, Toyota 39). All brands now use the same month (previously a Sep/Aug mix). Brands with no published figure (Tesla, Alfa Romeo) use the industry's 87 days, flagged. L = loan stress: percentile rank (0–1) across the 67 models of a tier-adjusted 60+ day delinquency rate (by loan balance), from 412,865 active loans in the Sep 2026 SEC ABS-EE loan-level filings of 15 auto ABS trusts (AmeriCredit 2024-1, Santander Drive 2024-2, Drive Auto 2024-1, Exeter 2024-2, CarMax 2024-2, Carvana 2024-P2, Ford Credit 2024-A, Hyundai 2024-A, Nissan 2024-A, Toyota 2024-A, Bridgecrest 2024-1, Ally 2024-1, World Omni 2024-A, Capital One Prime 2024-1, Honda 2024-1). Why adjust: carmakers' own prime lenders (Ford, Honda, Hyundai, Nissan, Toyota) hold most of their brands' loans, which would make those brands look safer just because of who financed them. Method: trusts are grouped into three lender tiers (subprime; prime non-captive; prime captive). Within each tier where the vehicle has ≥200 loans, its relative risk = (observed + 5) ÷ (expected + 5), where observed/expected are delinquent loan-equivalents at the vehicle's vs. the tier's overall rate (the +5 shrinks small samples toward 1). Relative risks are averaged with equal tier weights (not by loan counts), and the adjusted rate = average relative risk × the equal-weight average of the three tier-wide rates (prime captive 0.44%, prime non-captive 1.61%, subprime 9.54%). R = repo share: same method on repossessedIndicator. Vehicles with no tier ≥200 loans use their make's adjusted figures (flagged); a make with none gets the median (flagged). Raw pooled rates stay in each "Why?". Limits: results depend on which trusts were sampled (2024-vintage pools); equal tier weights are a choice, not a measured US lending mix; small samples remain noisy; and delinquency reflects who lenders approved for each car, not only the car itself. Westlake, GLS and Flagship trusts are 144A deals with no public ABS-EE, so they can't be added. LoanTape's make-level recovery flags are kept as context text only. A = affordability proxy (1 − normalized newest-model-year new price, CarEdge). This assumes cheaper cars have more subprime buyers; Experian shows subprime is 20.6% of used and 6.88% of new financing (Q1 2026) but has no figures by model. Price direction: ↓↓ score ≥60, ↓ 45–59, → below 45. Loan APR/term and incentive data are not inputs.
